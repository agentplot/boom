## Why

A project says things about itself in many places: an invariant in a
rules file, a decision in an ADR, an assumption in a planning note, a
boundary in a context map. Each is written once, and they drift apart.
An agent that reads one never sees the others, and nobody can say who
agreed to which. boom keeps all of them as one ledger of claims, records
who proposed each and who accepted it, and computes every other form
from that ledger.

## Who it is for

- **A developer working with coding agents**, who wants each decision
  and invariant written once and read by every agent that touches the
  code.
- **A project where agents propose and people decide.** An agent
  proposes claims as it plans; only a person accepts one.
- **A project that keeps ADRs in MADR.** `boom import adr` takes them
  in, and boom writes them back out as it records new decisions.
- **A project whose merges should keep the promises it made.**
  `boom check` runs in the merge gate beside the tests.

## Where it stands

boom is designed and not built. Its repository holds its rules, its
first decision record and a binary that prints its version. Every page
here shows what boom will do, and the [roadmap](/boom/roadmap/) lists
what the design has settled and what it has yet to settle.
